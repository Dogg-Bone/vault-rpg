# Verbatim Excerpts

## Core Book
### The Woodland (Pages 13-20)
The Woodland comprises thick, deep forests with plenty of resources for an enterprising empire, divided only by clearings and by paths. A clearing is a big area that the denizens of the Woodland have previously cut from the forest's grip, now one of the important spaces playing home to denizen life in the Woodland. All clearings are fairly built up, their contents ranging from large villages to small cities.

Everything changed some time ago when the denizens began to work together, creating clearings within the Woodland where they could live together in safety. They cut back the forest and turned some trees into buildings. They tamed the growth until there was a boundary past which the Woodland could not encroach.

Each clearing was more or less on its own—not because denizens wouldn't help each other, but because each clearing was dealing with its own problems. The paths themselves became more dangerous and overgrown as clearings traded only with their closest neighbors, and most turned their attentions inward to focus on local problems and politics.

Through it all, the Woodland survived. The clearings rebuilt themselves even as they formed their own new structures and ways of governing themselves.

## Travelers and Outsiders
### Clearing Formatting (Pages 221-245)
*(Format drawn from Heartwood and Sundew Bend examples)*
**Description:** A general overview of the clearing's history, current state, and the overarching mood or problem.
**At First Sight:** 2-3 paragraphs describing the immediate sensory details, architecture, and current activities visible to newly arriving vagabonds.
**Conflicts:** Clearings are beset by many troubles. One is the *Core Conflict*, while others are side conflicts. Each conflict includes factions involved, what they want, and "How it Develops" (what happens if the vagabonds never arrive).
**Special Rules:** Custom mechanics (often using PbtA-style moves) unique to the clearing's environment, resources, or factions.
**Introducing the Clearing:** How vagabonds might enter, who they might meet first, and escalations (GM moves designed to intensify the situation).
**Important Residents:** Statblocks and descriptions for key NPCs involved in the conflicts.

## RPG PHD Notes
### Atmosphere, Shape Flow & Effort States
- **Chekhov & Scenes:** Pick one atmosphere word (e.g., melancholy, feverish, suffocating). Choose details and pacing based on your word. Use two to three sensory details, drop a few cues, and then move on. Determine the psychological gesture of the environment (e.g., pulling deeper, closing in) and the energy quality of NPCs (pressing, gliding, slashing).
- **Laban & Shape Flow:** Track power over time. Identify the kind of power (authority, fear, influence) and how it moves (does it rise, spread, condense, fracture, stagnate, or sink?).
- **Effort States:** Give a location or faction a shared emotional language (Press, Flick, Slash, Glide, Wring, Punch, Float, Dab). Let that effort state shape everything: enemy behavior, civilian reactions, and the pacing of dialogue.

## High-Yield / "Lazy" Location Prep (Best Practices)
- **Evocative over Exhaustive:** Focus on 3-5 fantastic, memorable details rather than mapping every room or writing pages of history. Give the location a strong sensory footprint.
- **Actionable Secrets & Clues:** Prepare 10 secrets or clues associated with the location or its inhabitants that the characters can discover organically, regardless of where exactly they go.
- **Dynamic Situations, Not Static Plots:** Outline the current situation (who is doing what and why) and the impending escalation. Let the characters' actions dictate the plot rather than scripting the outcome.
- **Reusable Elements:** Focus your prep time on creating elements that are likely to be used—key NPCs, a few highly interactive set pieces, and a flexible random encounter or event table tailored to the location's theme.

## Moldy (Stratified) Worldbuilding
### Concepts
"Moldy worldbuilding" (also referred to as 'stratified worldbuilding' or 'palimpsest worldbuilding') refers to environments where the current state of the world is built upon the remnants of the past, characterized by multiple layers or strata of rebuild, growth, evolution, and decay cycles.
When generating stratified clearing ideas, ensure they incorporate both the physical layered environments (such as ruins or architectural strata) and how the current factions or inhabitants are actively interacting with or fighting over those layers.

# Layers of a Clearing

When designing a clearing for Root: The RPG, treat it as an "encyclopedia entry crossed with a storyboard" rather than a dry stat block. A clearing is a living, breathing pressure cooker of competing factions, environmental hazards, and desperate denizens.

## Layer 1 - Foundation & Geography (The Bones)
This layer defines the physical reality of the clearing. Where is it, what does it look like, and what secrets are buried beneath it?
- **Atmosphere & Effort State:** Pick one core atmosphere word and a guiding Effort State (e.g., Press, Glide) that dictates the general rhythm and emotional feel of the location.
- **Architecture & Footprint:** Rely mainly on narrative descriptions focusing on 3-5 evocative details (high-yield prep). What does the clearing look and feel like?
- **Moldy / Stratified History:** What came before? How is the current clearing built upon the ruins, infrastructure, or remnants of previous eras? How do the current denizens interact with these historical layers?
- **Shape Flow:** Track what kind of dominant power exists in the clearing (e.g., political control, fear) and how it moves (spreading, condensing, stagnating).
- **At First Sight:** What are the immediate 2-3 sensory details vagabonds notice upon arrival, reflecting the Atmosphere and Shape Flow?

## Layer 2 - Factions, Control & Conflicts (The Muscle)
This layer defines who is in charge and the pressures tearing the clearing apart.
- **Control & Dominant Inhabitants:** Which faction currently holds power (or claims to), and what is the dominant species makeup of the denizens?
- **The Core Conflict:** The primary problem driving the clearing's narrative. What is happening right now, who is involved, and what happens if the vagabonds do nothing?
- **Side Conflicts:** 1-2 additional, intersecting problems that complicate the situation.

## Layer 3 - Encounters & Interaction (The Nerves)
This layer covers the actionable elements for the players. Who can they meet, what unique things can they find, and what goes wrong?
- **Important Locations:** Specific buildings, ruins, or districts within the clearing.
- **Notable NPCs:** The key players in the conflicts. Give them character, distinct quirks, mannerisms, and flavorful backgrounds. Use the standard Root NPC layout.
- **Special Resources & Rules:** Mundane, non-magical items, environmental hazards, or unique mechanics represented by PbtA-style custom moves (using 2d6, 10+, 7-9, 6-).
- **Rumors, Secrets & Hooks:** Prepare actionable secrets or clues (high-yield prep) that draw vagabonds into the dynamic situations of the clearing.
- **First Session Encounters (2d6):** A random table of immediate events that tie directly into and reflect the clearing's established primary problems or conflicts.

# Fractal Clearings (Massive / Multi-District)

For massive, multi-district clearings (referred to as "fractal clearings"), a single template is insufficient. The overarching clearing must be defined, but its sheer size means each district functions almost as a clearing of its own.

When generating a fractal clearing, you must create:
1. **The Overarching Clearing Profile:** Details the macro-level control, the massive unifying conflicts that affect all districts, and the overarching history.
2. **Nested District Profiles:** Generate full clearing details (using the standard template below) for *each individual nested district*.
   - Ensure the details, NPCs, and conflicts of each district intersect logically with the macro-conflicts of the overarching clearing and the neighboring districts.
