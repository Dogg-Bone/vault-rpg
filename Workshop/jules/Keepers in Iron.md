# Keepers in Iron

An ancient, monastic order of heavily armored knights, returning from exile to reclaim the forgotten artifacts of the Woodland. Dogmatic, heavily armed, secretive, and unyielding.

### History
Long ago, the Keepers in Iron resided within the Woodland, serving as guardians of powerful relics, sacred sites, and ancient lore. When the Woodland fell into prolonged chaos—perhaps during the great civil wars of the [[Eyrie Dynasties]] or before—they were forced into exile, retreating to lands far beyond the borders of the forest to preserve what little they could carry. Over generations in exile, the order militarized and became deeply dogmatic, viewing themselves as the true custodians of the woodland's history. They transformed from scholars and guardians into an elite caste of badger crusaders clad in heavy iron armor, fiercely devoted to their singular cause.

Now, word has reached them that the Woodland is fractured once more, torn apart by the [[Marquise de Cat]], the Eyrie, and the [[Woodland Alliance]]. Seeing this as the prophesied time to return, the Keepers in Iron have marched back into the Woodland. They do not seek political dominance or territorial expansion like the other factions; they seek to recover the lost relics of their ancestors, viewing all current inhabitants as squatters living amidst sacred ruins.

### Flavor and Operations
The Keepers in Iron operate more like an expeditionary force than a conquering army. They construct heavily fortified, albeit temporary, waystations around active excavation sites. Their operations are methodical and highly disciplined: scouts locate ruins, heavily armored retinues secure the area (often by force, evicting anyone in their way), and specialized delvers unearth the relics.

To the average denizen of the Woodland, the Keepers are an intimidating, enigmatic presence. Their armor is ornate, massive, and entirely distinct from the functional steel of the Marquisate or the traditional arms of the Eyrie. They rarely engage in the politics of the clearings unless it directly interferes with their excavations, in which case they respond with overwhelming, uncompromising force. They view the woodland's current inhabitants with aloof pity or righteous disdain, believing that only the Keepers are worthy of holding the forest's ancient power.
