# 5-Room Dungeon Spark Tables

This document provides 2d6 spark tables to generate themes, rooms, and gameplay mechanics for 5-Room Dungeons set in the Woodland. Because these tables use a 2d6 roll, the results are distributed on a bell curve. Results toward the middle (6-8) represent more common, grounded occurrences, while results at the extremes (2 and 12) represent exotic, dangerous, or bizarre situations.

To build your dungeon, roll on the Theme table, then roll on the respective Room Type and Interesting Things tables for each of the 5 rooms. Finally, roll once on the Twist/Plot Hook table to tie the narrative together.

## Dungeon Theme (Roll 2d6)
| 2d6 | Theme |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | **Ancient Pre-Dynastic Ruin:** A bizarre, overgrown subterranean structure filled with forgotten traps and strange relics of an unknown civilization. |
| 3 | **Corrupted Grove:** A natural formation twisted by foul elements or dark rituals, inhabited by massive, feral beasts. |
| 4 | **Lord of Hundreds Mouse-Warrens:** A chaotic, sprawling network of tunnels occupied by a splinter group of Lord of Hundreds mice hoarding stolen wealth. |
| 5 | **Abandoned Eyrie Roost:** High in the canopy or carved into a cliff face, this former stronghold is filled with crumbling masonry and rusted armor. |
| 6 | **Marquisate Logging Camp / Mine:** An industrial worksite, recently overrun or abandoned, littered with half-finished structures and heavy machinery. |
| 7 | **Woodland Alliance Hideout:** A hidden cellar or cave system, meant for secretive meetings but recently compromised. |
| 8 | **Bandit Den:** A natural cave or old cellar repurposed by local scoundrels to stash their ill-gotten goods. |
| 9 | **Forgotten Shrine:** An ancient place of worship, quiet and dusty, where the air feels heavy with the scent of old incense and lost prayers. |
| 10 | **Vagabond's Stash:** A heavily booby-trapped series of hidden rooms created by a legendary Vagabond who never returned. |
| 11 | **River Trade Post (Flooded):** A partially submerged series of docks and storehouses, requiring careful navigation and avoiding water-dwelling hazards. |
| 12 | **The Bear's Lair:** A massive, terrifying den inhabited by one of the Woodland's apex predators, a true terror of the forest. |

## Room 1: Entrance / Guardian
### Room Type (Roll 2d6)
| 2d6 | Entrance Room Type |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | **Complex Lock / Vault Door:** An ancient, over-engineered barrier requiring specific mechanical knowledge or a unique key to pass. |
| 3 | **Collapsed Tunnel:** A tight, suffocating squeeze through a recent cave-in. |
| 4 | **Hidden Trapdoor:** Concealed beneath debris or a false floor, dropping straight into danger. |
| 5 | **Heavily Guarded Checkpoint:** A makeshift barricade manned by watchful guards (e.g., Marquisate conscripts or Bandit sentries). |
| 6 | **Natural Cave Mouth:** A simple, unadorned opening, perhaps obscured by thick brambles or vines. |
| 7 | **The Grand Doors:** Large, heavy doors, slightly ajar, showing signs of recent forced entry. |
| 8 | **The Decayed Portcullis:** Rusted iron bars that must be forcefully lifted or squeezed through. |
| 9 | **The Bridge/Chasm:** A precarious crossing over a steep drop before the true entrance. |
| 10 | **The Guard Beast:** A chained or sleeping creature (like a feral badger or large hound) blocks the way. |
| 11 | **The False Entrance:** An obvious, trapped entrance next to the smaller, true hidden path. |
| 12 | **The Counterweight Wall:** A massive section of rock or wood that appears solid but rotates seamlessly when a specific hidden counterweight is shifted. |

### Interesting Things (Gameplay Effect) (Roll 2d6)
| 2d6 | Gameplay Effect |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | **Toxic Spores:** The air is thick. Anyone breathing without protection marks 1 Exhaustion. If they push through, they must *Trust Fate* to avoid marking 1 Injury. |
| 3 | **Crumbling Architecture:** The entrance is highly unstable. Using heavy weapons or explosives here causes a collapse; anyone caught in it suffers 2 Injury. |
| 4 | **Alarm Snare:** A hidden wire or pressure plate. Triggering it immediately raises the alert level, changing the fictional positioning for the next room. |
| 5 | **Slippery/Steep Incline:** Requires careful climbing. A failed roll means a character drops an item, marking 1 Depletion. |
| 6 | **Echoing Chamber:** The acoustics amplify every sound. Attempting a stealthy approach here is significantly more difficult, requiring a *Trust Fate* roll. |
| 7 | **Recent Campfire:** Still smoldering. The ashes contain a half-burned map or journal, granting an advantage on the next *Read a Tense Situation* roll. |
| 8 | **Thick Brambles/Webs:** Requires hacking through. Weapons used to clear the path mark 1 Wear. |
| 9 | **Terrifying Omen:** A grisly warning or gruesome display. Any character who looks closely must mark 1 Morale or hesitate. |
| 10 | **Concealed Stash:** A hidden nook containing 1d4 Value worth of common supplies, but requires time to uncover. |
| 11 | **Magnetic / Strange Rock:** Metal weapons and armor feel incredibly heavy here. Characters in heavy armor mark 1 Exhaustion to move quickly. |
| 12 | **Sentient/Carnivorous Flora:** The vines actively grasp at intruders. Anyone caught must mark 1 Injury or 1 Wear (their choice) to tear free. |

## Room 2: Puzzle / Roleplaying Challenge
### Room Type (Roll 2d6)
| 2d6 | Puzzle Room Type |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | **The Automated Sentinel:** An ancient clockwork construct or pre-recorded mechanism that demands the correct physical tokens or passwords. |
| 3 | **The Mechanism:** A massive, complex machine of gears and levers that controls the door forward. |
| 4 | **The Parley:** A rival group (e.g., Lord of Hundreds mice) is currently resting here. They don't want a fight, but they block the path. |
| 5 | **The Weight Puzzle:** Pressure plates that require precise weight distribution to keep the exit open. |
| 6 | **The Locked Gate & Key:** A sturdy door with a missing component hidden somewhere in the messy room. |
| 7 | **The Chasm Crossing:** A broken bridge or series of precarious pillars requiring teamwork to navigate. |
| 8 | **The Symbol Door:** A locked door requiring the correct sequence of faction symbols to be pressed. |
| 9 | **The Wounded Explorer:** A denizen from a rival faction is trapped or injured here. Helping them opens the way but costs resources. |
| 10 | **The Pitch-Black Maze:** A confusing, dark area requiring a light source and careful mapping. |
| 11 | **The Mirror Room:** A puzzle requiring the redirection of light or reflection to reveal the exit. |
| 12 | **The Shifting Architecture:** The room physically rotates or changes layout periodically, confusing any sense of direction. |

### Interesting Things (Gameplay Effect) (Roll 2d6)
| 2d6 | Gameplay Effect |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | **Amnesia Gas:** Characters must mark 1 Morale. If they don't, they temporarily forget a Skill or move until they rest. |
| 3 | **Acidic Slime:** Coats important puzzle elements. Handling it without tools causes 1 Injury. Using tools causes 1 Wear to the tool. |
| 4 | **Time Limit:** The ceiling is slowly descending or water is rising. If the puzzle isn't solved quickly, all characters mark 1 Exhaustion to escape the trap. |
| 5 | **Distracting Whispers:** The room plays tricks on the mind. Any attempt to *Read a Tense Situation* is penalized. |
| 6 | **Fragile Floor:** Running or jumping causes the floor to break. Characters must move slowly, taking more time and risking discovery. |
| 7 | **Helpful Scrawlings:** Previous victims left clues. Grants a bonus to *Figure Someone Out* or *Read a Tense Situation* regarding the puzzle. |
| 8 | **Sticky Sap:** Everything in the room is coated in a strong adhesive. Retrieving items requires marking 1 Depletion (rations/solvents) or immense effort. |
| 9 | **Extreme Cold/Heat:** The temperature is unbearable. Characters without appropriate gear mark 1 Exhaustion upon entering. |
| 10 | **Valuable Mechanism:** The puzzle itself is made of precious metals. Characters can dismantle it for 2d4 Value, but doing so destroys the easy path forward. |
| 11 | **Gruesome Diorama:** Perfectly preserved remains or ancient murals depict a terrible tragedy. Witnessing it forces characters to mark 1 Morale, but provides crucial lore about the dungeon. |
| 12 | **Extreme Magnetism Zone:** A massive lodestone deposit. Any metal weapons or tools are violently pulled away and rendered unusable in this room. |

## Room 3: Trick / Setback
### Room Type (Roll 2d6)
| 2d6 | Trick Room Type |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | **The Hallucinogenic Spores:** A massive cloud of ancient fungi causes severe paranoia and hallucinations, turning the party against each other. |
| 3 | **The Trapdoor / Chute:** A sudden drop that separates the party, sending one or more characters sliding into different, smaller side rooms below. |
| 4 | **The False Treasure Room:** A room filled with glittering, worthless glass and a hidden ambush. |
| 5 | **The Ambush Point:** Snipers or hidden attackers use murder holes to attack the party with impunity. |
| 6 | **The Pit Trap:** A classic concealed pit, perhaps filled with stakes or muddy water. |
| 7 | **The Dead End:** A collapsed tunnel that forces the party to backtrack or dig their way through. |
| 8 | **The Poison Dart Hall:** Pressure plates trigger a volley of projectiles. |
| 9 | **The Rolling Boulder:** A large, heavy object is dislodged, forcing a desperate sprint to safety. |
| 10 | **The Flooding Room:** Doors lock behind the party as water rapidly pours in. |
| 11 | **The Captive's Cell:** A prisoner is here, begging for release, but they are actually a spy or a trap trigger. |
| 12 | **The Shifting Gravity:** The room orientation suddenly changes, turning walls into floors and dropping characters. |

### Interesting Things (Gameplay Effect) (Roll 2d6)
| 2d6 | Gameplay Effect |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | **Sabotaged Loot:** A valuable-looking item that is actually a rigged explosive. When taken, it detonates; the bearer must *Trust Fate* to avoid 2 Injury. |
| 3 | **Blinding Flash Trap:** Triggering this forces characters to mark 1 Injury (eyes) or act blindly (severe fictional disadvantage) for the next encounter. |
| 4 | **Rust Monster / Corrosion:** A trap or creature here targets metal. Any metal weapon or armor exposed to it immediately marks 2 Wear. |
| 5 | **Thief in the Shadows:** A nimble creature attempts to steal a random item. If successful, the character marks 1 Depletion. |
| 6 | **Slick Mud/Oil:** The floor is treacherous. Any physical exertion requires a *Trust Fate* roll to avoid slipping and falling prone. |
| 7 | **Discarded Equipment:** The trap has claimed others. Characters can find a useful item (like a rope or torch), restoring 1 Depletion. |
| 8 | **Noxious Fumes:** The trap releases gas. Characters must mark 1 Exhaustion to hold their breath while dealing with the setback. |
| 9 | **Loud Alarm Bell:** Triggering the setback alerts the boss in Room 4. The boss will be fully prepared and cannot be surprised. |
| 10 | **Magnetic Floor:** Metal armor sticks to the floor. Characters must remove their armor (losing its protection) or mark 1 Exhaustion per move to rip it free. |
| 11 | **Paralyzing Venom:** The trap uses a special toxin. An affected character cannot move their legs, requiring others to carry them (causing Exhaustion for the carriers). |
| 12 | **The Memory-Wipe Gas:** A strange chemical vent causes temporary, intense localized amnesia. The affected character cannot use their highest-rated skill until a rest is taken. |

## Room 4: Climax / Boss
### Room Type (Roll 2d6)
| 2d6 | Climax Room Type |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | **The Assembly Line:** The boss is desperately trying to start a massive, destructive pre-Dynastic war machine that must be sabotaged before it activates. |
| 3 | **The Precarious Ledge:** The fight takes place on a narrow bridge or crumbling balcony over a massive drop. |
| 4 | **The Throne Room:** An opulent, albeit decaying, chamber where the leader of the faction holds court. |
| 5 | **The Forges:** A blazing hot, loud room filled with machinery, providing plenty of cover and environmental hazards. |
| 6 | **The Boss's Lair:** A large, open cavern or grand hall, customized to the boss's fighting style. |
| 7 | **The Command Tent / Hub:** The central planning area, littered with maps and guarded by elites. |
| 8 | **The Arena:** A sunken pit or enclosed space designed for gladiatorial combat. |
| 9 | **The Treasury:** The boss fights amidst piles of loot, using the environment (tipping chests, throwing coins) as weapons. |
| 10 | **The Flooded Chamber:** Water is waist-deep, slowing movement and making dodging difficult. |
| 11 | **The Nest:** The massive creature's sleeping quarters, littered with the bones of past victims. |
| 12 | **The Colossus:** The "room" is actually the back of a massive, slowly moving creature or a giant mechanical construct. |

### Interesting Things (Gameplay Effect) (Roll 2d6)
| 2d6 | Gameplay Effect |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | **Frenzy Spores:** The air makes everyone aggressive. All melee attacks deal +1 Injury, but characters must *Trust Fate* to avoid attacking an ally if they miss. |
| 3 | **Boss Shield Generator:** An ancient device protects the boss. It must be destroyed (causing 1 Wear to weapons used against it) before the boss can be harmed. |
| 4 | **Minion Spawners:** Holes in the walls continuously pour out lesser enemies. Characters must spend actions (and Depletion) blocking them, or be overwhelmed. |
| 5 | **Environmental Weapon:** A ballista, crane, or chandelier can be interacted with. A successful roll deals massive damage to the boss but destroys the object. |
| 6 | **Hostages:** The boss has captives. Using area-of-effect attacks or reckless behavior will harm the hostages, causing severe fictional consequences with factions. |
| 7 | **Medicinal Font:** A pool of rare, naturally occurring healing sap. A character can spend an action to apply it, clearing 1 Injury or 2 Exhaustion. |
| 8 | **Destructible Cover:** The room is filled with pillars or crates. Taking cover absorbs 1 Injury, but the cover is destroyed in the process. |
| 9 | **Unstable Ground:** Every time the boss takes damage, a part of the floor collapses. Positioning becomes extremely important to avoid falling. |
| 10 | **The Boss's Flaw:** A glaring weakness (e.g., a blind spot, a rusted armor joint) is obvious. Exploiting it grants a significant advantage on the next attack. |
| 11 | **Empowering Relic:** The boss holds a powerful item. If a Vagabond can disarm them (a difficult *Trust Fate* roll), they can use it against the boss for the rest of the fight. |
| 12 | **Mutagenic Hazard:** Pools of strange liquid. If pushed into them, a character or the boss immediately mutates, gaining a random new physical trait and clearing all Exhaustion. |

| 2d6 | Reward Room Type |

| 3 | **The Ancient Vault:** A perfectly sealed metallic room holding pre-Dynastic technological marvels. |
| 4 | **The Prisoner's Cell:** The "treasure" is a high-value political prisoner whose freedom will anger one faction but endear another. |
| 5 | **The Secret Armory:** Racks of high-quality, perfectly preserved weapons and armor. |
| 6 | **The Treasure Hoard:** A classic pile of coins, gems, and valuable trinkets. |
| 7 | **The Safe Haven:** A secure, hidden room perfectly suited for a long rest, containing fresh water and edible mushrooms. |
| 8 | **The Smuggler's Stash:** Crates of contraband goods, valuable but highly illegal in nearby clearings. |
| 9 | **The Map Room:** A detailed layout of the Woodland, revealing hidden paths or faction troop movements. |
| 10 | **The Monument:** A statue or carving that holds a single, incredibly valuable jewel or relic. |
| 11 | **The Portal/Escape Route:** A fast, safe way out of the dungeon that leads to an unexpected clearing. |
| 12 | **The Sleeping God:** A massive, ancient creature in hibernation. The "reward" is the ability to harvest a scale/feather without waking it, or the terrible choice to wake it up. |

### Interesting Things (Gameplay Effect) (Roll 2d6)
| 2d6 | Gameplay Effect |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | **The Tainted Wealth:** The most valuable item is coated in a dangerous, addictive pre-Dynastic chemical. Taking it clears 1 Exhaustion immediately, but the character automatically marks 1 Exhaustion at the start of every session until it is disposed of. |
| 3 | **Trapped Chests:** The treasure is secured. Opening it incorrectly causes 1 Wear to the character's armor or ruins half the loot. |
| 4 | **Heavy Haul:** The loot is extremely bulky. Carrying it out requires marking 1 Exhaustion per character, and they cannot run if ambushed on the way home. |
| 5 | **The Decoy:** Half the treasure is fake. Characters must spend time and roll to *Figure Someone/Something Out* to distinguish the real valuables from the fakes. |
| 6 | **Perfect Rest:** The room is so calming and secure that taking a rest here clears all Exhaustion and Morale for the entire party. |
| 7 | **Signature Weapon:** Amongst the loot is a unique weapon with a special tag (e.g., *Armor-piercing*, *Far-reaching*). |
| 8 | **Faction Ledger:** A book detailing bribes and blackmail. Taking it grants a massive advantage (+1 ongoing) to social rolls with a specific faction. |
| 9 | **Incomplete Set:** The reward is part of a larger, incredibly valuable whole. It's worth little now, but acts as a plot hook for the next adventure. |
| 10 | **Complex Clockwork Item:** A pre-Dynastic weapon or tool with intricate, automated moving parts. It functions brilliantly but requires complex, rare oils to maintain, forcing the Vagabond to seek out a specific tinkerer. |
| 11 | **The Self-Destruct:** Taking the main artifact triggers a countdown. The party must flee immediately, ignoring all minor loot, or be crushed. |
| 12 | **The True Heir:** A document or artifact proving one of the Vagabonds is the rightful heir to an ancient Woodland title. |

## Twist / Plot Hook (Roll 2d6)
| 2d6 | Twist |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | **The Real Enemy:** The boss you killed was actually keeping something much worse trapped in the dungeon. By killing them, you've unleashed it on the Woodland. |
| 3 | **The Betrayal:** The patron who hired you to clear this dungeon is waiting outside with a small army to take the loot and silence you. |
| 4 | **The Infection:** One of the Vagabonds contracted a strange, slow-acting disease or fungal infection from the dungeon environment. |
| 5 | **The Rival Adventurers:** Another group of Vagabonds arrives at the treasure room at the exact same time, from a different entrance. |
| 6 | **The False Premise:** The dungeon was empty. The "monsters" were just scared denizens hiding from the real threat outside. |
| 7 | **The Marquisate Patrol:** A large Marquisate patrol notices you leaving the dungeon and demands a "tax" on your findings. |
| 8 | **The Structural Collapse:** The dungeon begins to collapse entirely. Escaping is a harrowing skill challenge. |
| 9 | **The Alliance Sabotage:** The Woodland Alliance rigged the dungeon to explode to kill a specific faction leader, and the Vagabonds are caught in the middle. |
| 10 | **The Collapsed Path:** When you exit the dungeon, you realize the tunnel behind you has completely caved in, and the path you took to get here has been wiped out by a massive landslide. You are lost in the deep woods. |
| 11 | **The Lord of Hundreds Horde:** A massive horde of Lord of Hundreds mice swarms the area just as you finish the dungeon, forcing a desperate retreat. |
| 12 | **The Eyrie's Claim:** An Eyrie Dynasties commander declares the entire ruin belongs to them. Keeping any loot means becoming an enemy of the Eyrie. |
